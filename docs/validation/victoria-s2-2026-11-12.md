## Root causes and fixes

Each fix has a focused Node check (`scripts/test_*.mjs`, Node 22, primitives only). CI picks these up
through its `scripts/test_*.mjs` loop.

| Failure | Root cause | Fix | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Victoria `s2-1`: failure to yield to `ambulance_1` at 87.7 s (Blanshard Street, `e405`) | The car turned right into the curb lane of a three-lane one-way street while clearing a junction for the ambulance. The route's lane was the leftmost, two lanes over, because of a left turn further on. The pull-over therefore aimed 7.5 m right of that lane's centre. Rules scored lane error from the lane's centre, which cost the pull-over 2 × 7.5 and cancelled its whole −15 bonus. Rules dropped it for a stop in the route's lane, and the car swung left. A road-closure re-plan at 73.7 s then set a new stop 8 m ahead, and the car stopped there 0.3 m left of the curb lane's centre. It stood there for 10 s while the ambulance came up behind and passed. The audit is right: the car never pulled over. | `brain/rules.js`: a pull-over's lane error is measured from where it pulls over to, not from the centre of the route's lane. A pull-over within the lane costs what it did before. Emergency vehicles are off in the bare world, so Rules changes nothing there. | `test_emergency_pull_across.mjs` |
| Victoria `s2-2`: stuck to the timeout (Menzies Street, `e545`, 465 s, 4 deadlock recoveries) | Menzies Street is a dead end, and the route U-turns at its end node. The server gives every turn's `at_m` as a sum of edge lengths. Over this 1 km route, with its rounded corners, that sum ran 15 m past where the route's polyline actually turns round. The turnaround code (`turnaroundStopS`, `planTurnaround`, the 12 m offer in sensing) reads `at_m` as arc length along the polyline. So the car stopped to turn round past the polyline's turn and drove into its ~2 m-radius hairpin. Every candidate was then off road, and after the deadlock creep the car stood there until the timeout. No other traffic was involved. | `map/route.js`: the `Route` places each U-turn where its polyline passes the U-turn's node, as `laneEdgeStarts` already measures. Other turns, and the route data, are left as they were. | `test_turnaround_drift.mjs` |
| Kitsilano `s3-1`: 3 deadlock recoveries, standing still for the first 12 s (Arbutus Street, `e607`) | The drive starts one lane left of the route's lane. A route starts where the car is, so `car_33`, standing 51 m behind in the right lane, projected onto the route's first point. Lane sensing (`laneOptions`) therefore counted it as alongside. The change into the route's lane was never clear, every other candidate had to return to that lane, and Rules held a hard brake until the deadlock creep moved the car. | `brain/sensors.js` `laneOptions`: a vehicle projecting onto either end of the route is placed along the line the route starts or ends on, so one behind the start is behind the car. | `test_lane_side_route_start.mjs` |

## Open

- Turn positions other than U-turns still come from summed edge lengths, so `nav.turn_in_m` can drift
  from the polyline by metres on long routes. Nothing here fails because of it. Moving every turn onto
  the polyline (in `Route` or in `jev/routing.py`) would change the bare suite, so it is left as it is.
- Right after a turn, a lane law decided before the turn names its lane relative to the route's lane.
  Where the route moves across lanes soon after the turn (`route_ahead`), sensing places the car in the
  route's lane even when it has turned into the curb lane. This is what made the pull-over's offset
  7.5 m in `s2-1`. The Rules fix makes the pull-over win anyway; the lane bookkeeping itself is unchanged.
