# Privacy and limitations

The server binds to `127.0.0.1`, keeps the API key out of the browser and checks hosts, origins,
fetch metadata and session tokens. A per-run budget and rate limit guard live API spending; the budget counts an endpoint without prices or a reported cost as $0.
TypeSafe receives the decision state and questions shown in the inspector: distances, speeds,
street names and manoeuvre descriptions, without map coordinates. Other decision models receive
the same request, and LLM endpoint drivers the same information as a chat prompt, from whichever
provider you configure. Drive reports stay in this
browser's local storage unless you export them. The public demo serves the published results and
curated replays as static files. Its free drivers send no model decision requests, and its
benchmark and arena saves stay on the current browser origin.
[Demo history and exports](../browser-demo.md#keep-runs-and-reports)

Map tags and procedural geometry limit realism. There is no road elevation or camber, and
visual body pitch and roll do not add a physical suspension model. Buildings, weather and darkness
determine sensing; a vehicle in traffic hides a pedestrian behind it, and so, with hidden hazards on, does one
standing at the kerb; with them on, road vehicles also hide other vehicles and cyclists (cars parked in a parking lane do not). Trees occlude nothing, and individual street lamps do not extend
detection range. Scenery collision checks use polygon exteriors and circular trunk/post cross-sections; courtyard holes and crash damage are not modeled. The driven car changes lanes only to pass and
then returns, and to get into the lane for a turn; two or more lanes from its route's lane, it keeps room for one lane change per lane. Where its route makes a U-turn at a dead end, it stops and turns round with a three-point turn (more legs where it is tight), letting its bumpers reach no more than 0.2 m over the kerb and keeping clear of parked cars and scenery; it does not turn round anywhere else, and the router uses U-turns only at dead ends. Where a street's lanes change at the junction
before a turn (a turn bay, say), the car still moves into the turn lane inside that junction. Turning left or making a U-turn at a signal, a stop sign or a junction without either, the car yields to the oncoming traffic it can see, waiting at the line or inside the junction, and it takes an oncoming car's left indicator at its word when that car is in its leftmost lane within 40 m of the junction (anywhere else, it may be a lane change; a car pulling out from the curb is waited for whatever it signals). It turns right on red after a full stop (except on the island of Montréal, judged from the map's centre against a coarse outline of the island), but only where it can see along the street to its left from the line: at a red light it does not creep forward to look, so at a corner a building hides it waits for the green, as it does in fog, rain, snow and after dark. Mapped red-turn bans apply with `redTurnSigns` on ([red-turn signs](red-turn-signs.md)); only supported relation tags are read, so the map is not a complete roadside-sign inventory. With `leftOnRed` on, one-way-to-one-way lefts on red are supported in the reviewed map regions outside Québec ([left turns on red](left-on-red.md)). At a two-way stop, once its stop is made, the car gives way to traffic on the through road from both sides; at a junction without a sign or signal it gives way to traffic from the right, or to the larger road where a smaller one meets it (road classes from OpenStreetMap: primary and secondary, then tertiary, then the rest). OpenStreetMap misses some stop signs, so a few junctions that have one on the street are treated as having none, and a T-junction follows the same rules as a crossroads. Where a building hides a street it gives way to, the car slows, creeps forward to look and goes once stopped at the point it looks from (0.3 m short of the crossing road's asphalt) if nothing it can see is coming, even if a building still hides part of the street; in fog and after dark it looks only as far as it can see. Traffic gives way by the same rules but does not look: it knows where everyone is. The audit counts pulling out in front of a vehicle with the right of way that is due at the junction within 2 s as a failure to yield, seen or not, if it was already on its way when the car crossed its line. Traffic does not overtake cyclists in the lane: on a street with one lane each way it stays behind one riding in the lane unless passing cyclists is on (`passCyclists`, free drives and the "Give them room" challenge), when it passes through the oncoming lane once that is clear (sim/cyclist-pass.js); parking uses forward pull-ins, and pedestrians do not cross
against signals. Street-name and speed-limit posts block the car; their labels do not add sensing information. No post or trunk stands in a lane or on the asphalt: where map topology would put one there (a slip road leaving a main road at a shallow angle, or a street continuing wider past a junction), it moves outward, a signal pole to the stop line's curb if the far side has no room, and otherwise is left out (across the twelve maps: 4 of 1,127 signal poles, 27 of 13,358 lamp posts, 11 street-sign and 2 stop-sign posts, and 4 trees).
Low curbs remain traversable, and scenery beyond the playable map is decorative with simplified geography.
